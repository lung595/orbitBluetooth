# Roadmap

Ideas, not promises, and no dates. Anything that would need the network or send data somewhere is out of scope, except an opt-in option that is off by default and sends the bare minimum (like *Real device pictures*).

## Planned

- **An option to swap the two volumes' roles**: DMS's volume slider would set this PC's level, and the device's own level would be set in Orbit only.
- **Listen together, further**: the first version ships in 1.14.0 (two to four outputs, one arc each). Still open: measuring each output's real latency so the delay is found by itself (today `togetherDelay` is set by ear), and sharing the level when the output you hear has no filter of Orbit's.
- **Wired outputs in Listen together, further**: the group's general volume (the ring and the speaker that mutes it, at the center) does not yet move a wired output beside a Bluetooth source that has Orbit's PC filters, so a muted group can still play through the wired one. The filter that holds a wired output back when it is the one you hear has still to be confirmed on real hardware. A wired square could glide into place when the group changes.
- **The center, further**: an IPC command for the general volume (`dms ipc call orbitBluetooth togetherVolume <percent>`), the source's own level reachable from the ring, a measured CPU cost per action, and a clearer way to tell whether sound really plays than PipeWire's link state.
- **The sky's depth, further**: a real bokeh (stars as soft bright discs) needs a small shader, because the effect's brightness only adds light; a blur per body for the host's system, instead of one veil, where the wallpaper cannot be blurred; a curved "planet limb" horizon under the group, as if it stood on a planet; and the cost of the glow and the veil, to measure in the real shell at 240 Hz.
- **Learned groups, further**: tuning the 30-day half-life and the one-minute threshold by use (each is one constant), once there is real use to look at. Salted hashes are left out on purpose: a salt in the same settings file adds little, and anywhere else would be written outside Orbit's own settings.
- **Hiding, further**: the menu of a wired member of a group still offers *Hide*, which only explains, so it could go; the *Hidden* section could open by itself after something is hidden; and the group menu with hidden outputs, and the black hole at two depths, still need a render check.
- **Try the whole lot in the real shell**: the gauge, the group icons, hiding from the group menu, the learned groups, the sky's depth, the battery arc colors, the dashed copies and their trajectory, Escape on the desktop widget and the volume scope's paint guard are checked with tests and off-screen renders only: still to see with a real pointer, and to measure (CPU per action, 240 Hz).
- **Volume with four fingers on a touchpad**: on a laptop, swipe four fingers up or down to raise or lower the volume, with the same smart steps as the keys (a slow swipe moves by 1 %, a quick one further) and the volume scope in the island. To check first: whether niri lets a four-finger swipe reach a plugin (today its touchpad gestures are its own), or whether reading the touchpad would need access Orbit should not ask for.
- **Two PCs with Orbit side by side**: two computers running Orbit near each other could recognize each other, for example to send one's sound to the other. Nothing is decided. It would be opt-in, off by default, found on the local link only (no Internet, no telemetry), with a safe pairing, and could share ground with *Listen together*.
- **The new-headphones pop-up on the nearest PC only**: when several computers run Orbit, each one would open the pairing sheet for the same new headphones. Only the PC they are most likely meant for should show it: the nearest one, probably the PC of the person who owns them. Nothing is decided; privacy and security come first. Each PC would judge on its own, from what it already sees locally (the headphones' signal strength as its own adapter reads it while discovering, and whether the screen is on, unlocked and in use), so no PC talks to another one, nothing is sent, and there is no message to forge, flood or replay; a PC that cannot tell keeps today's behavior. Should two PCs ever need to agree, that would be an opt-in, off by default, on the local link only, between PCs paired on purpose, exchanging only a short-lived salted hash (never an address or a name), every field checked and capped, so that a forged message could at worst hide a sheet, never pair or connect anything. The sheet only offers a device, it never pairs it by itself. The rule is written and tested, and the study is in [docs/nearest-pc-study.md](docs/nearest-pc-study.md); it needs the signal strength, which Quickshell does not expose in 0.3.1 (see *Distance from the signal strength*), so reading it from BlueZ and wiring it into the pop-up is the next step.
- **Pause when you take your headphones off, for other brands**: it ships for Sony headsets with a wearing sensor. AirPods, Galaxy Buds, Nothing, Huawei and the others each report wearing in their own vendor message, which needs a verified, freely licensed description (or a capture from a real headset) before Orbit sends anything: no packet is guessed.
- **Conversation length for Samsung**: Sony ships. The Galaxy Buds message is only described in a GPL-3.0 project, and Orbit is MIT and copies no code, so it waits for a description it can rely on.
- **Confirm the Sony wearing and conversation messages on a real headset**: they follow the MIT-licensed SonyHeadphonesClient (the wearing sequence was confirmed on a WH-1000XM6 there), but Orbit has not yet seen them answer on a headset of its own.
- **3D models of devices** (to animate them): Sketchfab only lets a signed-in account download a model, which Orbit cannot ask for without an account and a token. Previews (pictures) are what ships today.
- **Easier to read code**: split the largest files (`OrbitScene.qml`, `DeviceBody.qml`) by role, without changing behavior.
- **More headphones tested on real hardware**: only Sony, Huawei and Nothing Ear (2) have been tested so far; reports for other brands are welcome.
- **Distance from the signal strength**, once Quickshell exposes it (it does not in 0.3.1).

## Not planned

- **Firmware updates**: they usually need the vendor's app and the network.

## Known limits

- *What really plays*: the bit rate is only shown where the codec makes it known (LDAC, aptX, aptX HD; never SBC or AAC), the latency is the PC's part only (the headset's own delay cannot be read), and the quantum only appears while the output is playing.
- *Pause when you take the headset off* keeps one control connection open, and a Sony headset accepts only one at a time: the Sony app on a phone may not reach it meanwhile. After an error Orbit does not retry until the headset reconnects, and a player is paused only when its names equal those of the stream that reaches the headset.
- *Ambient motion* only pauses when windows fill the whole screen: a single floating or narrow window placed over the widget does not pause it, because niri does not tell where tiled windows sit on screen. On compositors other than niri it never pauses.
- Some Sony headsets do not report charging, or drop Bluetooth while charging; this is a hardware limit.
- *Listen together* is built from how PipeWire and Bluetooth headsets are meant to work and tested on a simulated sound server; with a real multipoint headset it is the headset that decides which device it listens to when the PC and the phone both play. See [Works with multipoint headsets](docs/GUIDE.md#works-with-multipoint-headsets).
- *Wired outputs in Listen together* are checked with made-up outputs and a simulated sound server only: they have not been tried on real hardware (a USB interface, an HDMI screen, the jack), nor the chooser's real click-through and the cable and squares under a real pointer. The filter that holds back a wired output when it is the one you hear is not confirmed there, and a USB output that reports no latency counts as 0 ms (see [Wired delay](docs/GUIDE.md#wired-delay)).
- Next to a Bluetooth source that has Orbit's PC filters, the group's general volume gauge and its mute speaker do not move a wired output: a muted group still plays through it.
- A wired square jumps into place when the group's members or source change (no glide), vanishes without a fade when an output leaves; planets of the outer belt are not pushed away from it.
- The group chooser reads the wired outputs once, when it opens (a cable plugged in meanwhile shows the next time), and offers only the devices on the sky, not one in the black hole.
- The ghost group, with *Reduce motion*, rests at the top of a frozen ring, half behind this computer's core, and still answers clicks. It reads the plugged outputs again when the output in use or the connected devices change and whenever the orbit opens, not when a jack's port alone changes. The suggestions you turn down are forgotten when the shell restarts (memory only, by design).
- The sky's depth is not a true bokeh, and a learned group is stored as 32-bit hashes of its outputs: nothing readable and nothing leaves the computer, but not a cryptographic secret. Hiding from the group chooser, the gauge and the group icons are checked off-screen only. See the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) for the full list.
- A device of this computer's system that passes behind the group is hidden by it (only the group's own copies are drawn over the source, as dashed outlines); clicking this computer shows everything. Letting those devices show through and answer a click too is an idea, not decided.
- With *Reduce motion* nothing turns, so the first copy of a group rests behind the source, up and to the right of it, and stays a dashed outline for as long as the group lasts (it still answers clicks).
